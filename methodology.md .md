This project starts local on purpose. The first goal is to prove the concept in a focused region, beginning with Tennessee, Kentucky, and Indiana, and then expand. The long‑term goal is for every state, every county, and all relevant public data to be entered so that anyone within Takeda who chooses to use this tool can benefit from it.

The tool is designed to stay completely separate from company data. It does not pull from internal targeting lists, CRM exports, or paid commercial datasets. All information comes from public sources and field verification only. To avoid conflicts, we do not attach dollar amounts to any relationship signals. Instead, we use simple, qualitative indicators such as “Potential KOL activity” and the number and type of professional engagements.

The intent is not to rank people by money, but to understand who shows a consistent affinity for narcolepsy and related sleep disorders in our territories. By combining public provider information, public relationship signals, and field knowledge, the tool helps reps see which HCPs and centers are most likely to be important for narcolepsy NT1 care, while keeping the method transparent and compliant.
